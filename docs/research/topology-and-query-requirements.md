# Board Topology: Structural, Query, and Information-Set Requirements

The companion to `board-topology-rules-language.md`. That survey recorded what
~60 board, tile, and card games *are*; this document derives what the language
must *support* to host them: the minimal taxonomy of topology structures, the
query language over them, and how each interacts with the projection model
from which information sets are derived. It is a requirements analysis — it
characterizes constructs semantically and deliberately proposes no surface
syntax. Where a choice existed between a new construct and a reduction to
existing machinery, the reduction won; the count of genuinely new things is
kept as small as the corpus allows.

## TL;DR

- **The structural core is two things, not seven.** One new primitive: the
  **graph** — finite positions, typed edges, optional region labels, optional
  generated adjacency (grids are graphs whose edges come from a coordinate
  formula). One reused primitive: the **zone at a position** — a graph
  position's occupant is an ordinary zone (single slot, stack, or multi-node
  footprint), so per-observer projections attach to board cells with no new
  visibility machinery. Everything else surveyed — tracks, tessellations,
  tile segments, covering DAGs, stacking, emergent kingdoms — is these two
  plus a property or a query.
- **Most tracks are not topology.** Cribbage pegging, scoring tracks,
  rondels, and Snakes & Ladders reduce to integer state the language already
  has. Racko's rack and Bohnanza's queue are ordered zones, not boards. Only
  backgammon, Parcheesi-family branching, and racing lanes keep real
  positional structure.
- **The query language needs one new value type (Position/PosSet) and about
  a dozen operators**, organized by the survey's ten computation classes.
  Fixed-point work (flood fill, reachability) terminates by construction as
  built-in primitives bounded by the finite position count — the Ludii/GDL
  resolution — so the expression surface stays non-recursive while results
  include fixed points. Optimization queries (longest road, Arboretum paths)
  are NP-hard in general and admissible only behind a declared cardinality
  bound; absent the bound they are a static error, per surface totality.
- **Hypothetical evaluation ("legal iff P holds after this change") is a
  modality, not a query**, underlying Quoridor walls, Hive's one-hive rule,
  castling, and legal-move generation generally. Its load-bearing constraint:
  hypotheticals are emission-free by construction, or legal-move enumeration
  would leak hidden state into information sets.
- **Of the nine hidden-information × topology patterns, three are FREE today,
  three reduce to existing machinery, and six candidate capabilities survive
  scrutiny** — ranked: per-position zone families (foundational, presupposed
  by everything spatial-hidden), probe actions (Battleship/Cryptid/Clue),
  then four single-witness axes to defer under the corpus-first discipline
  (attribute-level projections, transition-projections, deferred reveal,
  anonymous-persistent identity).
- **Tigris & Euphrates is free on the info-set axis** — the surprise result.
  Emergent kingdoms are a pure public function of public placements; the
  hidden VPs are an ordinary hidden accumulator zone. The requirement is a
  discipline, not a capability: derived zones carry no persistent
  observational identity.
- **Every board game in OpenSpiel's current catalogue fits the core** —
  verified against the live games list. The OpenSpiel games that remain hard
  (Kriegspiel, Reconnaissance Blind Chess) are hard for knowledge-model
  reasons already tracked in `generalization-path.md`, not topology.

## Key findings

1. **Grid ⊂ graph.** A grid is a graph whose edge set is generated by a
   coordinate formula (rank/file offsets, hex axial, 4/6/8-way) instead of
   enumerated. The only thing a grid needs beyond the bare graph is a
   declared coordinate system so rays and lines have axes to walk. This
   matches the survey's finding that connectivity kind is always part of the
   rule: the adjacency relation is a declared parameter of the structure,
   never a default.

2. **Occupancy is the existing zone model, attached to positions.** A board
   cell holding a stack (Hive beetles, NMBR 9 levels, backgammon points) is a
   graph node whose content is an ordered zone; a ship spanning four cells is
   one object with a multi-node footprint (the inverse: one object, many
   positions); Catan needs different occupancy on each of its three layers
   (productive hexes, single-occupant vertices, single-occupant edges).
   Reusing zones here is what makes visibility work for free — projections
   are already defined over zones.

3. **Three surveyed shapes reduce to graph-plus-property.** Tile-internal
   sub-structure (Carcassonne segments, Galaxy Trucker connectors) is the
   same graph machinery at sub-cell grain — a tile is itself a small graph,
   and placement glues graphs along identified boundary nodes. Covering DAGs
   (Pyramid, Mahjong solitaire) are directed edges plus an availability
   predicate aggregated over in-neighbors ("all parents removed"), with
   Mahjong adding a second lateral edge type ANDed in — typed edges, which
   Risk's sea lanes and Carcassonne's segment kinds already require.
   Emergent zones (T&E kingdoms) are the flood-fill query persisted and
   named — an incrementality requirement on a query, not a shape.

4. **The graph must be a mutable value acted on by closed kernel verbs.**
   Static boards (chess), grown boards (Carcassonne, Hive), added edges
   (Dots and Boxes), removed edges (Quoridor walls), removed nodes splitting
   regions (T&E), re-orientation as a move (Pentago), and bulk permutation —
   The Amazing Labyrinth's row shift is an atomic cyclic permutation of cell
   contents (tiles and the pawns riding them, the ejected tile becoming the
   new spare, plus a stateful no-immediate-undo constraint), not reducible
   to per-cell moves. Placement/removal
   legality can be an arbitrarily complex pure predicate, but the mutation
   itself must go through kernel verbs so observation events fire uniformly
   — the board-game instance of "extensions compute; only the kernel acts."

5. **Six cross-cutting properties configure the core**: occupancy model
   (single / capped / stack / multi-node footprint), boundedness (fixed
   extent, declared cap — absolute or floating — or bounded only by
   component count; see the boundedness regimes in Part I), per-position
   attributes (terrain, per-cell
   restrictions, promotion ranks — queryable by the same predicate language
   as occupancy), orientation (representable always, restrictable rarely,
   mutable post-placement only in Pentago), ownership/mirroring (per-player
   frames over one shared graph — "forward" as a declared per-player
   transform, not a second board), and mutability (finding 4).

6. **Termination never depends on state values.** Classes 1–4 of the
   computation catalogue are bounded by constant neighborhoods or board
   diameter; flood fill and reachability reach their fixed point in at most
   |positions| expansions because the visited set grows monotonically over a
   finite set; move-existence is bounded by the finite action space;
   hypotheticals add one shadow-state construction. Only the optimization
   class needs an explicit declared bound. This is what preserves the
   sub-Turing guarantee while admitting fixed-point results.

7. **Composition, not a flat menu.** Kingdomino is region-then-count
   (component size × crowns within it); Carcassonne farmers are two region
   partitions plus region-to-region adjacency between them; Quoridor is
   filter-then-reachability over a hypothetically mutated adjacency. The
   minimal algebra: position sets as first-class bindable values carrying
   the existing ZoneContents surface (`where`/`count`/set operations),
   predicates and connectivity as parameters, and region results nameable
   within one rule evaluation using the existing binding forms. Composition
   depth stays statically bounded because the call graph stays acyclic.

8. **Spatial queries over hidden zones are a leak channel.** A query whose
   result an observer can see must depend only on positions and attributes
   visible to that observer, or be routed through a declared projection —
   the spatial generalization of the existing concern that legality
   (`demands`) reading hidden zones leaks through the legal-move set. Flagged
   as a first-class lint requirement, not solved here.

9. **The projection model survives the challenge set with six candidate
   extensions, four of which should wait.** Per-position zone families and
   probe actions have broad forcing sets. Attribute-level projections
   (Stratego), transition-projections (Scotland Yard), deferred reveal
   (Fury of Dracula), and anonymous-persistent identity for moving hidden
   pieces (Stratego again) are each currently forced by a single witness —
   real requirements, honestly recorded, but premature to design for until
   second and third witnesses arrive.

## Part I: The structural core

### The graph

A finite set of **positions**; **typed edges** between them (adjacency may be
enumerated per game, like Risk's territories and sea lanes, or generated from
a declared coordinate system, like every grid and hex board); optional
**region labels** partitioning positions into named groups (continents,
scoring regions). Occupancy attaches independently to positions, edges, or
regions — the survey's three distinct occupancy targets (chess cells, Go
intersections, Ticket to Ride edges) plus region-only games with no edge
layer at all (El Grande). Catan's triple dual (hex/vertex/edge, each with its
own occupancy) is one graph with three position kinds and incidence relations
between them, not three boards.

Directed edges with in-neighbor aggregate predicates cover the covering-DAG
family: Pyramid's "exposed iff both parents removed" and Mahjong's
conjunction of vertical clearance with lateral freedom are availability
predicates over typed in-edges, evaluated exactly as Sagrada's negative
placement predicate is evaluated over neighbors.

Sub-cell grain covers the tile-internal family: where a game's features live
on segments smaller than a placed tile (Carcassonne's roads, cities, and
fields; connector types; through-lines), the board graph is declared at
segment granularity and tile placement glues boundary segments. The same
region and adjacency queries then work unchanged — the requirement is that
grain is per-game declarable, not that segments are special.

### The zone at a position

A position's occupant is a zone, reusing the existing container model:
single-slot (chess), capped multi-occupant (Tokaido's double spaces),
unbounded ordered stack (Hive beetles, backgammon points, NMBR 9 levels), or
a multi-node footprint where one object spans several positions (Battleship
ships, polyominoes). This is the pivotal reuse: per-observer projections are
defined over zones, so making cells zones is what lets the visibility model
extend to boards without a parallel mechanism (Part III, C1).

### What reduced away

Tracks: a path or cycle graph when they need structure at all (backgammon's
typed points, Parcheesi's ring plus per-player tails, racing lanes as lane ×
distance grids); a bare integer with a modulus and jump table otherwise
(cribbage, scoring tracks, rondels, Snakes & Ladders) — ordinary state
arithmetic, no topology. Rank and furthest-behind turn order are order
queries over player positions, not structure. Position-semantic sequences
(Racko's indexed rack, Bohnanza's FIFO hand) are ordered zones with indexed
access — card-DSL territory, out of the topology axis entirely. Emergent
zones are the flood-fill query persisted with identity (Part II class 6,
Part III pattern 9).

### The six configuration properties

Occupancy model, boundedness, per-position attributes, orientation,
ownership/mirroring, and mutability, as in Key Finding 5. None is a shape;
all must be settable on any graph or zone-at-position instance, and the
query language must treat attribute reads uniformly with occupancy reads.

Boundedness deserves one refinement: there are three bounding regimes for a
growing board, not two. (1) **Absolute declared extent** — a fixed region
set at setup, with in-bounds as part of placement legality. The forcing
witness is Tacta, whose rules promote the physical surface to game topology:
the edge of the table is the boundary of the play area, and no card may be
played past it — making the boundary a contestable resource (steer the
layout toward an edge to deny expansion room). An O(1) class-1 check
(`on_boundary` already covers it). (2) **Floating cap** — the played
region's bounding box may not exceed a declared size, anchored dynamically
by placements rather than absolute coordinates (Kingdomino's 5x5). Also
O(1) per placement. (3) **Component-derived bound** — no rule bounds
extent, but the finite piece supply does (Carcassonne's 72 tiles, Hive's 22
pieces bound the reachable diameter). Regime 3 is how the compiler honestly
derives the finite extent OpenSpiel's state space and fixed-size observation
tensors need anyway; regimes 1–2 are *rules* with strategic content and must
be designer-declarable surface, not compilation detail. Games whose
finiteness is physical but uncodified (Vye's growing tableau, played on a
table that runs out before the deck does) present a fidelity choice at
description time: a floating cap preserves the run-out-of-room strategy
without modeling where the start card sat, whereas defaulting to regime 3
would silently delete it — a choice the language should force the designer
to make explicitly.

## Part II: Query-language requirements

One new first-class value: **Position**, with **PosSet** (a set of positions,
carrying the existing ZoneContents algebra), **Connectivity** (always an
explicit parameter), position predicates (the existing `where`-closure
convention lifted to positions), and **Path** (an ordered PosSet retaining
walk edges, for queries that report length or cost).

### The operator inventory, by computation class

Class 1, adjacency and occupancy (O(1)): `neighbors`, `is_adjacent`,
`adjacent_to_any`, `occupant`/`owner`/`is_vacant`, `edge_compatible(tile,
pos, orientation)` (rotation folded in as a parameter, matching every
rulebook), `on_boundary`. Sagrada's negative constraint is composition
(`not adjacent_to_any(...)`), not an operator.

Class 2, rays and lines (O(diameter)): `line(pos, direction, stop_condition)`
— the workhorse for sliding pieces, runs, and through-lines; `ray_first_hit`;
`line_length` (Lines of Action's occupancy-derived range); `between(a, b)`
(castling, pass-through triggers); `run_count(pos, axis)` (Azul).

Class 3, fixed-shape patterns (O(1) per anchor): `matches_shape(pos,
template, orientation)`, `count_shapes`, `fully_covered(region)` — templates
are declared static shapes, so matching is a constant-size scan.

Class 4, track arithmetic: `advance(pos, delta, policy)` with policy ∈
{exact_landing, overshoot, bounce}; static jump lookups; per-player branch
remapping; `rank_among`/`furthest_behind` as order queries.

Class 5, path existence (O(V+E)): `reachable(from_set, to_set, via_filter,
conn)` — Hex's win, destination tickets, wall legality, hidden-mover
candidate filtering.

Class 6, connected components (O(V+E)): `region(pos, same_by(attr), conn)`
(flood fill from a seed); `liberties` (a composition: vacant neighbors of a
region); `component_count`; `largest_component`; `is_enclosed` (closure
tests); multi-seed flood with a visited set (Pandemic's outbreak, stated in
the rulebook as exactly this algorithm). All work at whatever grain the board
declares, including sub-cell segments.

Class 7, optimization (search; gated): `longest_path` (Catan, with
opponent-cut edge filters; Ticket to Ride's trail), `cheapest_path` (Power
Grid, TransAmerica's missing-track scoring), `best_scoring_path` (Arboretum's
ascending same-species paths), `max_capture_chain` (international draughts).

Class 8, move-existence: `any_legal_move`/`no_legal_move` — checkmate,
Amazons, Mahjong stuck-detection. Cost |moves| × cost(legality), and legality
may nest classes 5–6 and 10.

Class 9, region cross-referencing: adjacency between two independently
computed partitions (Carcassonne farmers). Decomposes into two class-6
partitions plus pairwise region adjacency — composition, not new machinery.

Class 10, the hypothetical modality: `holds_after(mutation, predicate)`.

### Termination

Classes 1–4 are trivially bounded. Classes 5–6 are fixed-point computations
whose termination is guaranteed by the finite position count — so they are
built-in primitives owning their own loops, never user-written recursion,
exactly as Ludii and the GDL family handle connectivity. Class 7 is the
honest hard edge: longest-path is NP-hard in general, but corpus instances
are small (roads ≤ 15 edges, trains ≤ 45, grids of a few dozen cells) — the
language must demand a **declared cardinality bound** (piece or edge counts)
under which the compiler admits the query; absent a bound it is a static
error with a clear message, never a silent slow path. Class 8 is bounded by
the static action space. The governing principle: no operator's termination
depends on state *values*, only on finite position and move counts.

### Composition

PosSets are first-class bindable values with the ZoneContents surface plus
set operations; predicates and connectivity are parameters everywhere; region
results are nameable within one rule evaluation via existing binding forms
(Carcassonne must compute both partitions once and relate them). Named
functions factor composed queries under the existing hermetic, acyclic-call
guarantees. No new scoping machinery.

### The hypothetical modality

`holds_after` constructs a shadow state by applying a tentative mutation
drawn from the closed kernel verb set, evaluates a pure predicate against it,
and discards it. It underlies not just Quoridor walls but legal-move
generation generally ("does this move leave my king attacked" is
`holds_after` per candidate; class 8 is `holds_after` iterated). Hive's
during-transit connectivity requires the mutation to present intermediate
substates, each checked. The load-bearing requirement: **shadow evaluation is
emission-free by construction** — queries are pure, only real kernel verbs at
real sites emit observations — otherwise enumerating legal moves would leak
every hypothetical into information sets.

### Purity and the leak channel

Every spatial query is a pure, deterministic function of state (same regime
as existing derived functions; the byte-identical swap proofs depend on it);
flood-fill frontier order must be deterministic so results replay. The
flagged constraint (Key Finding 8): a query over contents the observer cannot
see, whose result the observer can see, is a leak — it must either read only
observer-visible inputs or route its result through a declared projection.
This belongs to the same lint family as "legality reads only
observer-visible information."

### Cost budget

Queries run inside legal-move enumeration, so per-move cost multiplies by the
action space. Classes 1–6, 9, 10 are polynomial and admissible
unconditionally; class 7 only behind its declared bound. The corpus's worst
nesting is class 8 wrapping class 10 wrapping class 5 (Quoridor: every
candidate wall re-runs reachability for every player). Purity makes
per-(state, query) memoization safe within an enumeration pass — a permitted
runtime optimization, not a language feature.

### Designer legibility

Each operator names a recurring rulebook phrase from the survey's vocabulary
table: `neighbors` is "adjacent/touching"; `line` is "straight line";
`reachable` is "path/route to"; `region` is "territory/kingdom/group/chain";
`is_enclosed` is "surrounded/no gaps"; `longest_path` is "longest road";
`holds_after` is the conditional-invariant idiom ("there must always remain
one path to the goal", "may not pass through an attacked square", "must
remain connected at all times"). The query surface can sound like the
vocabulary column rather than a graph library.

## Part III: Information-set interaction

Tested against the model as it stands: hidden information lives only in zone
contents (state is public); zones assign each observer one of six
projections; observation events are emitted from movements and projected per
observer; the OpenSpiel adapter builds each observer's information state from
the projected stream alone. For each of the survey's nine hidden-information
× topology patterns, the question is whether the hidden variable is
zone-shaped and whether the required observation is a projection of a
movement.

**Free with today's machinery** (given the topology axis itself): hidden
timing/resources (pattern 1 — hands and decks, unchanged); synchronous
pre-commitment reveal (pattern 2 — `expose_top`/`reveal` at the decision
site); hidden goals over public topology (pattern 3 — a private objective
zone plus a pure query over public state at scoring; the query emits nothing
because it reads nothing hidden); and — the notable result — emergent zones
with hidden accumulators (pattern 9, T&E): kingdom membership is a pure
public function of public placements, recomputable identically by every
observer, and the hidden VPs are an ordinary hidden accumulator zone fed by
public events. The requirement is a discipline: **derived zones must not
carry persistent observational identity — observations key to the underlying
public placements**, so merges and splits cannot desync recall.

**Reduces to existing machinery:** owner-excluded visibility (pattern 8,
Golf/Skyjo) is an unusual-but-legal observer→projection map (the owner gets
`existence_only`; peeks grant per-cell `identity to owner`) plus an
actor-blind per-move override (the mover's source projection is `trivial` —
the mover does not learn what it moved). Nothing new in the core; the
requirement is that library aliases never hardcode "owner sees own zone".
Role-conditioned payloads (Clue's three-tier fan-out) reduce to the existing
per-observer override once probe actions exist, with the observer→projection
map computed from the probe outcome. Hidden payoff at a public location
(pattern 4, waterholes) is the existence-vs-identity split the lattice
already makes, once positions are zones.

**The six surviving capability candidates, ranked:**

- **C1. Per-position zone families** — foundational; forced by every
  spatial-hidden pattern (4, 5, 6, 7, 8). A board is an indexed family of
  position-zones over a declared topology sharing a projection policy
  (declaring 200 cells one at a time is untenable). Projections are defined
  over zones; therefore spatial hidden state requires positions to be zones.
  Highest leverage; presupposed by everything below.
- **C2. Probe actions** — forced by Battleship, Whitechapel's questions,
  Cryptid, Clue. An action whose effect is evaluating a designer-written
  predicate over a hidden zone's true contents and announcing the result to
  a declared (possibly outcome-conditioned) observer set. Not a movement —
  nothing moves; the existing `announce` is the emission half, the probe is
  the missing evaluation half.
- **C3. Attribute-level projections** — Stratego's colour-public/rank-private
  split is not expressible when projections act on whole content objects;
  the escapes (colour as public state, colour as zone owner) break when
  pieces move. This is the already-flagged pose/axis-2 requirement. Single
  witness today (Luzhanqi would be the second) — defer.
- **C4. Transition-projections** — Scotland Yard's edge-label emission is a
  function of the traversal, not of either endpoint's contents; the current
  model has no channel for move-property observations. Single witness —
  defer.
- **C5. Deferred/conditional reveal** — Fury of Dracula's encounter cards:
  a reveal whose trigger is detached from the creating movement and partly
  player-gated. `reveal` exists; the decoupled trigger does not. Single
  witness — defer.
- **C6. Anonymous-persistent identity for hidden pieces** — Stratego's
  opponent must track *which* unknown piece moved (accrued rank knowledge
  follows it), but identity continuity is currently observed only when both
  endpoints are identity-visible, so hidden-to-hidden moves unlink. The
  movement event must carry a stable anonymous reference. Same latent need
  as tracking a face-down card as "the same unknown card", so it may
  generalize beyond boards. Single spatial witness — defer, but watch for
  the card-side witness.

**Implementation-ordering signal.** FREE (no new info-set capability): all
perfect-information board games, the public-topology network games, nearly
all tile-layers, the solitaire family, and T&E. CHEAP (one capability):
Battleship/Cryptid via C2; Golf/Skyjo via the C1+alias discipline; Clue via
C2 plus its fan-out generalization. EXPENSIVE (multiple): Stratego
(C1+C3+C6), Scotland Yard (C1+C4), Fury of Dracula (C1+C4+C5 plus
sliding-window history — the ceiling), full Whitechapel (C1+C2+C6 over heavy
topology). Land C1 first, then C2; defer C3–C6 until their single-game
forcing sets gain the second and third witnesses the corpus-first discipline
requires.

## Part IV: Coverage

The two-primitive core plus properties and queries covers every game in the
survey. Spot checks at the extremes: Carcassonne = graph at segment grain +
growth mutability + class-6 regions + class-9 cross-referencing; Mahjong
solitaire = typed directed edges + conjunction availability predicate;
backgammon = path graph + zone-at-position stacks + class-4 policies;
Stratego = grid + C1/C3/C6 on the knowledge axis; T&E = growth/removal
mutability + persisted class-6 components + ordinary hidden zones. The games
needing *no* topology at all (cribbage's board, scoring tracks, Racko,
Bohnanza, Klondike's cascades) confirm the corpus-first instinct: a large
fraction of apparent "boards" are zones and integers the language already
has.

Cross-checked against OpenSpiel's live games catalogue: every board or
spatial game there (the chess/Go/Hex family and variants, Amazons, Clobber,
Lines of Action, Havannah, Y, Pentago, TwixT, Nine Men's Morris, Xiangqi,
dark/phantom variants, Banqi, Chinese Checkers, Mancala/Oware, Dots and
Boxes, Quoridor, Hive, backgammon, Parcheesi-family, Battleship, the
gridworld RL testbeds, 2048, Morpion Solitaire) reduces to the same core —
grids as generated graphs, reserves and pits as zones, rotation and merging
as kernel mutations. Banqi and Dark Hex land on the same knowledge
capabilities as Stratego (C1+C3+C6) and phantom-move games
(Kriegspiel, Reconnaissance Blind Chess, Phantom Go) remain gated on the
attempt-feedback knowledge pattern already recorded in
`generalization-path.md` — a knowledge-model gap, not a topology gap. No
OpenSpiel game forced a sixth structural shape; the survey corpus
(Carcassonne, T&E, Taluva) is strictly harder than OpenSpiel's current
board-game set, which is the right way around for a target.

### Post-survey witnesses: word games

Scrabble and Upwords (checked after the survey; neither is in OpenSpiel).
Topology is fully covered: a fixed generated grid with per-position
attributes (premium squares, the centre star), racks and the bag as ordinary
hidden zones, word extraction as class-2 line reads (the maximal contiguous
run through the placed tiles, both axes, reading ordered contents along the
Path), scoring as arithmetic with per-cell multipliers. Upwords is the
zone-at-a-position stack reduction verbatim — words read from the top of
each cell's stack, scoring counts stack heights, and its
must-change-the-word rules are line predicates over top-of-stack contents. A
true 3D lattice variant only requires that grid generation not be hardcoded
to two dimensions.

Two genuinely new requirements: **bulk declared data** — legality hangs on
membership in a ~100k-word lexicon, a pure finite relation that fits the
expression model as a lookup but cannot be written inline, so surface
totality demands an external-table declaration form (Onitama's move cards
are the miniature of the same idea; TwixT's no-crossing rule — "your links
may not cross any other link, not even one of your own" — is a third
witness, a static precomputable conflict relation over candidate link
pairs, data rather than runtime geometry); and **atomic multi-placement
moves** — a turn places up to seven tiles as one action whose legality (all
cross-words valid, collinear, gaps filled by existing tiles) is a joint
predicate over the set, with a single observation event for the compound
move. Mancala-family sowing is the same compound-move shape with older
pedigree (Oware: sow one seed per house skipping the source; capture is a
bounded backward contiguous-run scan over the opponent's row taking houses
holding 2–3 seeds; the "must feed the opponent" rule resolves as a
legal-action filter and grand slams as capture suppression — class 2 scans
and legality filters, no new machinery). Tak's stack-distribution moves
(pick up k stones, drop along a line) are a third compound-move witness.

### Post-survey witnesses: collection spot-checks

Further mechanisms confirmed against rules and absorbed without new
structure: **attribute chosen at play time** — Cat in the Box's quantum
suits (a card has no suit until declared on play) and Scrabble's blanks are
two witnesses for object attributes fixed by a decision at the moment of
placement; Cat in the Box's shared research board adds a **claim-once
global uniqueness constraint** (each value-suit cell claimable exactly once
game-wide — kin to shogi's nifu as a global uniqueness predicate), and its
paradox rule ("no legal declaration exists") is a class-8 move-existence
check ending the round — a scoring-relevant use of `no_legal_move`.
Railroad Ink confirmed entirely within the core (edge-typed drawing,
connected-exit components, longest-run scoring), its only novelty being
simultaneity, a known kernel gap. Tsuro is the Carcassonne segment
machinery plus follow-the-path-to-its-end automatic movement (a bounded
class-2 traversal). Games that stress other, already-tracked axes rather
than topology: Sky Team (hidden-from-partner dice plus a communication ban
— the Hanabi hint-economy axis), the open-channel communication family — Codenames (the hidden key card is
a Cryptid-style private mapping over a public grid, patterns 3/6), Spyfall
(the location is a zone visible to every observer except one — the
all-but-one projection map is already legal, an inversion of the
owner-excluded case), and Noobs in Space (distributed hidden cards, each
identity-visible only to their holder) — where the hidden-information
skeleton is free in the projection model but the game lives in an
open-vocabulary natural-language channel (plus a real-time timer in
Noobs), both principled scope exclusions, Innovation (splay is a derived visible-icon count
over public tableaus — free; its dogma effects are the axis-4 CCG
horizon), Scythe (typed river edges, pattern-4 encounter tokens, pattern-3
hidden objectives; combat is simultaneous sealed bids — the Goofspiel
gap), and legacy/campaign layers (Pandemic Legacy, Gloomhaven) which are
rule mutation and persistence, explicitly deferred.

### The adversarial pass

A deliberate attempt to break the outline: a systematic sweep of
BoardGameGeek's full mechanics taxonomy (~190 entries) classified against
the core, plus rules-verification of eight suspected breakers (Root,
Sheriff of Nottingham, Trax, Tzolk'in, Tragedy Looper, 1830, the
Wallenstein cube tower, Blokus). The overwhelming majority of mechanics
land in covered / tracked-other-axis / excluded. What survived scrutiny:

**One load-bearing catch — `claim` is not `announce`.** Sheriff of
Nottingham's declarations ("only Legal Goods... the exact number of
cards") may be lies. The existing `announce` asserts a *true* fact and
narrows observers' candidate sets; modeling a false declaration as
`announce` would corrupt every derived information set. Bluffing games
need a **claim event**: public, common-knowledge *as an utterance*
("X declared bag=Y"), content-agnostic of truth, never intersected with
candidate sets — paired with ordinary hidden zones and conditional
reveals (Sheriff-only on inspection; coarser on pass; and a bribery side
channel that reveals bag contents outside the inspect/pass binary). Coup,
already in the corpus, is retroactively the first witness — its character
claims are exactly this shape — so the distinction has two witnesses and
should be stated wherever the observation model is specified.

**A tenth hidden-information pattern — derived public aggregate of hidden
spatial state.** Escape from the Aliens in Outer Space: a coarse public
zone (sector) whose occupancy is a *projection of* a hidden fine-grained
position. Pattern 4 (hidden payoff at public location) inverted: the
public layer is computed from the hidden one. This is the spatial
instance of axis-2 computed projections — a query over hidden contents
whose *result* is the declared observable — and it connects directly to
Part II's leak-channel constraint (this is the sanctioned version of that
leak).

**Refinements to recorded requirements, each verified against rules:**
18xx tile upgrades need an existential over a small finite orientation
set wrapping `holds_after` ("does any rotation preserve all connections")
— an exists-over-enum combinator or documented desugaring, still pure and
O(1). Trax's forced-tile cascades are a bounded deterministic fixpoint
(Pandemic-shaped) but with rollback: an illegal junction retroactively
invalidates the whole turn, so the cascade must run speculatively —
`holds_after`'s shadow-then-discard discipline applied to a chain of real
mutations. Tzolk'in's gears are Labyrinth's bulk permutation exactly,
plus one interface need: the permutation must expose per-cell prior
occupants to owner-keyed effect hooks (ejected workers pay their owner).
Blokus reduces to ANDed footprint-level connectivity predicates
(diagonal-required, orthogonal-forbidden, same-colour only) plus a
move-count-gated fixed-cell opening rule — compositions, not capabilities.

**Surface requirements, expressible today but unnamed:** Root-scale
faction asymmetry reduces to actor-conditioned legality under a shared
phase skeleton, *but* factions carry structurally distinct zone sets
(Eyrie's decree columns, Alliance's supporter stack) — a "faction module"
declaration unit is an ergonomics requirement, not a semantics one.
Games coupling two independent graph instances in one legality check
(market-row position × placement-board position) need queries to name
which board they range over — multi-instance topology should be stated
explicitly. Automa/solo bots are choosers bound to a rules-defined policy
function — the seat-binding list (human, LLM, solver) gains a fourth
member whose decision procedure is itself rules text.

**A modeling-fidelity case, kin to Vye's table:** the Wallenstein cube
tower is a stateful physical randomizer — ejection depends on
accumulation history, no probability model is stated, and near-capacity
it jams. Any digital treatment invents a distribution; like uncodified
table space, the language should force that invention to be an explicit
designer declaration rather than a silent default.

**A convergence flag:** Diplomacy-shaped secret written orders stack
three separately-deferred capabilities at once — simultaneity, deferred
reveal (C5), and transition-projections (C4). No single game currently
forces the combination, but any orders-based corpus candidate would, so
the three should not be designed in mutual ignorance.

**One honestly open edge:** enforceable contracts. Atomic agreed trades
(Chinatown) are ordinary kernel swaps, and honor-system deals (Diplomacy)
are utterances — both fine. But an engine-*enforced future obligation*
created by a joint decision (a binding contract restricting later
legality) is a kernel concept with no named home; recorded here as out of
current scope until a corpus game forces it.

**Confirmed reductions worth keeping as negative results:** Tragedy
Looper's time loops (reset is zone reinitialization; hidden roles are
assigned once; player knowledge is just the unerased observation
history); tech trees and stat-track thresholds (legality predicates over
monotone public state, plus the trigger registry for threshold-crossing
bonuses); closed-economy auctions (arithmetic over a shared finite pool,
fully public); discrete I-cut-you-choose (partition into zones, then an
ordinary chooser — the continuous version falls under the
continuous-quantity exclusion).

## What this does not decide

Surface syntax for any of the above; which witness games gate which
implementation stage (a roadmap question); whether C3–C6 ever get built
(each waits on witnesses); and the visibility-lint design for the spatial
leak channel (flagged in Part II, owned by the knowledge model). The next
concrete step this analysis supports: pick the first witness ladder game
(backgammon for tracks-with-structure, or a tile-layer for growth +
regions) and design the graph declaration + class 1–6 query surface against
it, with C1 as the visibility story from day one.
